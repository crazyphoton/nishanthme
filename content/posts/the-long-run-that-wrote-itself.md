Long runs are the price of wanting to run a marathon.

They are essential, but they are also incredibly boring. A full marathon might take me five hours. The training runs are slower, deliberately so. There is less effort spent pushing the pace or trying to hold on. This is good for the body, but not always for the mind.

Podcasts help. Mike Duncan’s *Revolutions* has probably carried me through more kilometres than any running shoe.

But I have recently noticed another problem. I consume far more than I synthesise. I read books, listen to podcasts and have interesting conversations, but much of it gradually disappears. I have been trying to write more, reflect more and make a conscious effort to turn consumption into thought.

Halfway through a long run, I had a fairly obvious idea.

A Zone 2 run is supposed to be conversational. So why was I not having a conversation?

I stopped the podcast and started talking to an AI.

We began by discussing the French Revolution episode I had just heard. The conversation gradually became an outline for a blog post. We then drafted the post itself. After that, we started discussing this post, about writing a blog post while running.

The surprising part was not that the AI helped me write. It was that the run almost disappeared.

I had intended to run around 10 kilometres. Instead, I had been running for nearly two hours and had hardly noticed. It felt less like exercise and more like a long phone call with a friend who asks useful questions.

The conversation also helped me pace myself. As long as I could continue talking comfortably, I knew I was keeping the effort under control. Talking became an incidental feedback loop for the run.

The workflow is not perfect. Voice is excellent for exploring an idea, but it is much worse for editing one. At some point, I still need to look at the words, move sections around and decide whether a sentence actually works.

For now, I may have found a way to solve the boredom of marathon training. Every long run can become an opportunity to think through something I have read, develop an argument and return home with the first draft of a blog post.
